## Facts

- **Date**: built at the end of the 2nd or the start of the 3rd c. AD. Coins from Septimius Severus (193–211) to Tacitus (275). In use about a hundred years; out of use by the end of the 3rd c. In the 14th c. craftsmen had workshops in the ruins.
- **Water**: an aqueduct came first. A Greek–Latin inscription (found 1851) says that under Antoninus Pius the city and the governor T. Vitrasius Pollio brought water to Odessos (believed AD 157).
- **Size**: the largest ancient building found in Bulgaria; "fourth-largest Roman thermae in Europe" (after Caracalla and Diocletian in Rome and the Imperial Baths of Trier).
- **Plan**: "small imperial" type, symmetrical about a north–south axis. Two bathing routes ran side by side, one in each half. Oriented to the cardinal points.
- **Rooms** (letters of Vacheva 2008, Fig. 8a): V, V′ vestibules at the NE and NW corners, entered from the north facade by three steps; A, A′ apodyteria (changing rooms), the largest rooms after the hall B; B the *basilica thermarum* (the big north hall, with exedrae); F frigidarium and T tepidarium on the axis, each with two pools; C caldarium in the centre of the south side, with a semicircular pool in the south apse; heated rooms I–III and I′–III′ along the south side, the corner ones (I, I′) with big apses; a row of shops along the north, east and west sides; a latrine in the SW corner, with its own entrance from the street.
- **Two floors**: the bath floor, and below it a half-sunk service floor. This floor has vaulted galleries, the drains and a 101 m long heating corridor (*praefurnium*) under the south facade with three exedrae, 6 furnaces, 2 boiler rooms, 6 draught openings and 4 chimneys. The ground slopes down to the south. Following Vitruvius, the heated rooms face south.
- **Heating**: hypocaust (raised floors), with hot air carried up in the walls. The corner rooms I and I′ were heated only from the next room, through vaulted passages in the walls (Vacheva 1985).
- **Construction**: *opus mixtum*, with bands of stone and brick. Vaults also in *opus mixtum* (Vacheva 1989). The south facade has a base of dressed stone blocks (*opus quadratum*, courses 0.5 m and 1 m high, the low courses standing out by 0.035 m) and a profiled cornice. The vault over the praefurnium forms a terrace along the whole south front.
- **Windows** (Vacheva 1985): six twin windows (*bifora*) in each south exedra, with ten-flute half-capitals. Each window has a stone grid of 0.42 × 0.42 m panes. The panes were glass (one double-glazed frame was found) or thin coloured marble (0.005 m). The window sills slope inwards.
- **Pools and fountains**: shallow marble "trays" without drains in front of the frigidarium and tepidarium pools, and a square one in the centre of the west apodyterium, under a *labrum* (fountain basin). All were faced with polished grey-white marble.
- **Decoration**: marble wall facing, granite and marble columns, Romano-Corinthian capitals, columns shaped like Hercules' club, statues (Hercules, Victoria/Nike, Mercury, Asclepius, Hygieia), mosaics.
- **Research**: E. Kalinka 1906; K. & H. Škorpil 1906–1921 (thought it was a Justinian acropolis; Karel Škorpil strengthened the "Roman Tower"); S. Pokrovski 1940; M. Mirchev 1959–1971 (main excavation); first measured plan by arch. Kamen Goranov, early 1960s; K. Vacheva from 1980 (new survey; levelling plans of both floors in 1985); photogrammetry by the national institute for monuments (NIPK), 1980s; P. Georgiev to 1992; N. Stamatova, 3D, 2006–2019.

## Where sources disagree / what is unknown

- **Area**: "over 7,000 m²" (museum, Wikipedia), "over 7,100 m²" (BTS), 7,420 m² excavated (Vacheva 2008).
- **Date**: "late 2nd c." vs "end of the 2nd / start of the 3rd c."; in use "about 100 years" vs "abandoned by the late 4th c." (one web page). The coin range (193–275) supports the shorter use.
- **Upper parts are hypothetical.** In 1985 only about a quarter of the south facade was excavated. The rest lies under the church of St. Atanas and modern houses. Vacheva gives variants for the stairs, the windows between the exedrae and the wall finish. The roofs and the east and west facades were left for "a separate study". Her monograph (1986) was never printed.
- **Shops "possibly brothels"** (museum page): speculation, do not state as fact.
- The identification of some small rooms (sudatorium, unctorium, destrictorium) is uncertain (museum page).

symmetrical from the **west half**, which is the better excavated one and the half the museum drawing shows.
